What is KBase?

KBase (Department of Energy Systems Biology Knowledgebase) is a free, open-source platform for collaborative systems biology research. Think of it as:

  • A computational workbench - Run bioinformatics analyses without installing software
  • A data repository - Store and share genomic and biological data
  • A collaborative platform - Work with colleagues on shared projects
  • An interactive notebook - Document your research like a lab notebook

KBase works through Narratives, which are interactive documents that combine data, analysis results, visualizations, and text - similar to Jupyter notebooks but designed specifically for biological research.

TipWhy Use KBase?
  • No installation required - Cloud-based, accessible from any web browser
  • Free compute resources - Run analyses without your own infrastructure
  • Reproducible research - Every step is documented and shareable
  • Collaborative - Share your work with colleagues easily

Creating a KBase Account

Step 1: Go to KBase Website

Navigate to https://kbase.us in your web browser.

Step 2: Sign Up

  1. Click the “Sign Up” or “Login” button in the top-right corner
  2. You have several sign-up options:
    • Google account (recommended for convenience)
    • ORCID (Open Researcher and Contributor ID)
    • Globus (research identity management)
    • Username/Password (create new KBase credentials)
Tip

Using Google or ORCID is convenient as it leverages existing credentials.

Step 3: Complete Registration

If using Google or ORCID:

  1. Click your preferred sign-in provider
  2. Authorize KBase to access your basic profile information
  3. Complete your KBase profile

If creating KBase credentials:

  1. Click “Sign up with KBase”
  2. Enter your email address
  3. Create a username (visible to other users)
  4. Create a strong password
  5. Agree to the terms of service
  6. Verify your email address

Step 4: Complete Your Profile

After signing up, complete your profile with:

  1. Real name - Your full name (optional but recommended)
  2. Email - Your email address
  3. Organization - Your institution or company
  4. Research interests - Brief description of your work
Note

Your profile helps others in the community understand your work and facilitates collaboration.

Accessing KBase

Logging In

  1. Go to https://narrative.kbase.us
  2. Click “Sign In”
  3. Choose your sign-in method (same as registration)
  4. Enter your credentials

The Dashboard

After logging in, you’ll see the KBase Dashboard with:

  • Narratives - Your interactive analysis documents
  • Data - Your uploaded and generated data
  • Search - Find public data and narratives
  • Account - Manage your settings

Understanding Narratives

What is a Narrative?

A Narrative is your digital lab notebook in KBase. It contains:

  • Data - Files and datasets you’re working with
  • Analysis cells - Tools and apps you run
  • Markdown cells - Your notes, explanations, and documentation
  • Output - Results from your analyses

Creating Your First Narrative

  1. From the Dashboard, click “New Narrative”
  2. Give your narrative a descriptive name (e.g., “My First Analysis - Nov 2025”)
  3. The narrative editor will open
TipNaming Best Practice

Use descriptive names that include your project and date: “ProjectName_Analysis_2025-11-10”

The Narrative Interface

When you open a narrative, you’ll see:

  • Top toolbar - Save, share, and manage your narrative
  • Left sidebar - Access to data and apps (collapsed by default)
  • Main panel - Your narrative cells (like pages in a notebook)
  • Data panel (left sidebar) - View and manage your data

Working with Data in KBase

Understanding Data in KBase

Data in KBase can come from:

  • Public reference data - Genomes, datasets shared by the community
  • Your uploads - Data you bring from your computer
  • Analysis results - Data generated by running apps

Viewing Your Data

  1. In your narrative, click the “Add Data” button (or the data icon in left sidebar)
  2. You’ll see tabs:
    • My Data - Data in your current narrative
    • Shared With Me - Data others have shared
    • Public - Community reference data
    • Import - Upload new data

Adding Public Data to Your Narrative

  1. Click “Add Data” → “Public” tab
  2. Use the search bar to find data (e.g., “E. coli genome”)
  3. Click on a dataset to view details
  4. Click the “+” button to add it to your narrative
  5. The data will appear in your Data Panel
Note

Public reference genomes and datasets are immediately available - no download or upload needed!

File Transfer and Upload

For Small Files (< 2 GB)

  1. Click “Add Data” → “Import” tab
  2. Select the type of data you’re uploading:
    • FASTA - Sequence files (genomes, proteins)
    • FASTQ - Raw sequencing reads
    • GenBank - Annotated genome files
    • And many others…
  3. Click “Select File” or drag and drop your file
  4. Configure import settings (name, description)
  5. Click “Import”
WarningFile Size Limits

The web interface works best for files under 2 GB. For larger files, see the Globus section below.

For Large Files (> 2 GB) - Using Globus

Globus is a fast, reliable file transfer system for large datasets.

Step 1: Set Up Globus

  1. Go to https://app.globus.org
  2. Sign in (you can use your institutional login or create a Globus account)
  3. Link your KBase account:
    • In Globus, search for “KBase” in the Collections search
    • Look for “KBase Bulk Data”

Step 2: Install Globus Connect Personal (for your computer)

  1. Download Globus Connect Personal from globus.org/globus-connect-personal
  2. Install it on your computer
  3. Sign in with your Globus credentials
  4. Set up your personal endpoint (give it a name like “MyLaptop”)

Step 3: Transfer Files

  1. In the Globus web interface:
    • Left panel - Select your personal endpoint (your computer)
    • Right panel - Select “KBase Bulk Data” endpoint
  2. Navigate to your files on the left
  3. Navigate to your KBase staging area on the right: /username/
  4. Select files on the left
  5. Click “Start” to begin transfer
  6. You’ll get an email when transfer completes

Step 4: Import from Staging Area to KBase

  1. Return to your KBase narrative
  2. Click “Add Data” → “Import” tab
  3. Look for “Import from Staging Area”
  4. You’ll see your Globus-transferred files
  5. Select the file and configure import settings
  6. Click “Import”
TipWhen to Use Globus
  • Files larger than 2 GB
  • Multiple large files
  • Faster, more reliable transfers
  • Automatic retry on network issues

Alternative: Using Command Line (iRODS/iCommands)

For advanced users comfortable with command line:

# Note: This requires installing iCommands on your computer
# Installation instructions: https://docs.irods.org/master/icommands/user/

# Initialize connection (one-time setup)
iinit

# Host: data.kbase.us
# Port: 1247
# Username: your_kbase_username
# Zone: kbase

# Upload a file to staging
iput -K myfile.fastq /kbase/staging/username/

# Check upload
ils /kbase/staging/username/

Organizing Your Data

Creating Folders in Data Panel

While you can’t create traditional folders in narratives, you can:

  1. Use descriptive names - Include information in the data object name
  2. Use multiple narratives - Organize by project
  3. Add metadata - Document what each dataset contains

Adding Metadata to Data

  1. In your Data Panel, hover over a data object
  2. Click the “…” menu
  3. Select “Edit metadata”
  4. Add descriptions and tags
  5. Click “Save”
Tip

Good metadata makes it easier to find your data later and helps collaborators understand your work.

Saving and Managing Narratives

Auto-Save

  • KBase automatically saves your narrative as you work
  • Look for the save indicator in the top toolbar
  • Recent changes are saved every few seconds

Manual Save

  • Click the Save icon in the top toolbar anytime
  • Use keyboard shortcut: Ctrl+S (Windows/Linux) or Cmd+S (Mac)

Narrative Versions

Each time you save, KBase creates a version:

  1. Click the clock icon in the top toolbar to see versions
  2. You can revert to previous versions if needed
  3. This is helpful if you want to undo major changes
Important

Always save before closing your browser or ending a session!

Sharing Your Work

Sharing a Narrative

  1. Click the “Share” button in the top toolbar
  2. Choose sharing options:
    • Private - Only you can see it
    • Shared with specific users - Enter KBase usernames
    • Public - Anyone can view it (read-only)

Setting Permissions

When sharing with specific users:

  • View - Can see but not edit
  • Edit - Can modify the narrative
  • Share - Can share with others
Warning

Be careful with Edit permissions - collaborators can change your work!

Making Your Narrative Public

  1. Click “Share” → “Make Public”
  2. Add a description of your work
  3. Your narrative gets a permanent URL
  4. Anyone with the link can view it
  5. Great for publishing methods or sharing with the community

Adding Documentation

Why Document?

Good documentation makes your work:

  • Reproducible - Others can understand and repeat your analysis
  • Understandable - You’ll remember what you did months later
  • Shareable - Collaborators can follow your process
  • Publishable - Can serve as supplementary material

Adding Text Cells

  1. Click “+ Cell” or “Add Cell” in your narrative
  2. Select “Markdown Cell”
  3. Write your documentation using Markdown formatting
  4. Click outside the cell or press Ctrl+Enter to render

Basic Markdown Examples

# Main Heading

## Subheading

**Bold text** and *italic text*

- Bullet point 1
- Bullet point 2

1. Numbered item
2. Another item

[Link text](https://kbase.us)

> This is a quote or important note
TipDocumentation Best Practices
  • Describe your data sources
  • Explain why you chose specific parameters
  • Note any issues or observations
  • Summarize key results

When asking for help:

  1. Share your narrative - Make it public or share with support
  2. Describe the problem - What were you trying to do?
  3. Include error messages - Copy the exact error text
  4. Mention your username - Helps support locate your work

Common Issues and Solutions

“I can’t find my data”

  • Check you’re in the correct narrative
  • Look in the Data Panel on the left
  • Data might still be importing (check status)
  • Check if data is in your staging area (Import tab)

“My upload is stuck”

  • Large files take time - be patient
  • Check your internet connection
  • Try using Globus for large files
  • Clear browser cache and try again

“I accidentally deleted something”

  • Currently, deleted data in narratives cannot be recovered
  • Always make copies of important data
  • Consider sharing important narratives with yourself as backup

“My narrative won’t load”

  • Try refreshing the page
  • Clear browser cache
  • Try a different browser (Chrome recommended)
  • Contact support if problem persists
